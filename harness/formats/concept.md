# Format: concept

**Use it for** a topic taught from zero: what something is, why it exists, and how its parts fit. For example: what a database transaction is, how an agent decides which tool to call, or what a harness hook does.

**Budget:** about 1,500 words per lesson. If a topic needs more, it's two lessons.

## The shape

1. **The header** (the rulebook's "Every lesson").
2. **In one paragraph:** what this is, and why it matters to what he's doing now. A reader who stops here should still know the point.
3. **Part 1, the big picture.**
   - What it is, in plain words, with one real example he can see or run.
   - Why it exists: the problem it solves, or what goes wrong without it.
   - Where it fits next to what he already knows. Name the link to his earlier work, if there is one.
4. **Part 2, one level down.**
   - The kinds or parts it comes in, each with a short real example and what goes wrong with it.
   - The patterns worth recognising, and where he'll meet them next.
5. **A prediction, before the reveal.** At least one place where he guesses what will happen before the lesson shows it. "Before you run [L03-B2], write one line: what will it print?"
6. **Questions:** at most 3, each pointing at the block it needs. Their answers are nowhere in the lesson.
7. **Parked:** what's left out on purpose, and why.

## Rules for this format

- **It stands alone.** Don't assume he read an earlier lesson. If something earlier matters, say it again in a sentence, or link to it.
- **Big picture first.** Numbers, tables of results and fine detail come only after the why.
- **Small real blocks.** Each example is something he can run or read in under a minute, with its real output. Never use an invented output.
- **Nothing ahead of the evidence.** Show the problem before the fix, and the observation before the explanation.
- **One idea at a time.** Each section adds one thing.

## Checklist before it goes out

- [ ] The opening paragraph alone says what and why.
- [ ] Part 1 comes before Part 2, and Part 2 is one level down, not a dump of detail.
- [ ] At least one prediction comes before a reveal.
- [ ] Every block is labelled, every command has been run, and every output is real.
- [ ] It reads without any earlier lesson.
- [ ] There are at most 3 questions, and none of their answers is in the lesson.
- [ ] Parked is there.
- [ ] It's within about 1,500 words.
- [ ] The linter passes.
- [ ] It's been re-read against his last reply. Does it answer what he asked, at the depth he asked?
